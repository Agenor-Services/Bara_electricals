## Why Heat in a Switchboard Means Trouble

Electrical current flowing through a properly sized, tightly torqued connection encounters negligible resistance and produces virtually zero heat.

When a connection becomes loose or corroded:
- Electrical resistance surges.
- According to Joule's Law ($P = I^2R$), heat increases exponentially with current.
- Temperatures inside the panel can rapidly exceed 400°C, melting terminal blocks and catching timber backboards on fire.

Our electricians use calibrated digital thermal cameras to pinpoint the exact overheating terminals and rectify the danger immediately.
