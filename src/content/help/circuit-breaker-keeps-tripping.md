## Circuit Overload vs Short Circuit: Understanding the Difference

A circuit breaker is a thermal-magnetic safety device designed to protect building wiring from overheating. When a breaker trips, it is doing its job — preventing electrical fires.

Understanding whether you have an overload or a short circuit dictates your next step:

### The Thermal Overload
When you run too many appliances at once, the total current draw slowly exceeds the breaker's rated limit (e.g. pulling 22 Amps through a 16 Amp breaker). The breaker heats up over several minutes until its thermal bi-metal strip flexes and trips the switch. 

**The fix:** Reduce the simultaneous load by unplugging non-essential heating appliances or have an electrician run a new dedicated circuit.

### The Magnetic Short Circuit
If an active wire touches a neutral wire inside an appliance or behind a wall plate, hundreds of amps surge through the circuit in a microsecond. The breaker's internal magnetic coil senses this massive spike and trips instantly with a sharp snap.

**The fix:** Do not turn the breaker back on until the faulty appliance or damaged wiring has been identified and isolated.
